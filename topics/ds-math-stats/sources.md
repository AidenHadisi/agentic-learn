# Sources — Math & Statistics for Data Science

Everything read while researching this topic. Append as you go; dedupe by URL.

- **Comparing Loss Functions** — EECS 245, 2023 · https://notes.eecs245.org/introduction-to-supervised-learning/comparing-loss-functions/
  University course notes — clean bridge from mean/median to MSE/MAE loss; good for the ML payoff of descriptive stats.
  Used for: §1
- **Expectations and Medians as Minimizers** — Gundersen, G., 2019 · https://gregorygundersen.com/blog/2019/10/04/expectation-median-opt/
  Practitioner math blog — rigorous-but-readable derivation of mean/median as L2/L1 minimizers; solid for the optimizer framing.
  Used for: §1
- **Introduction to Modern Statistics — Exploring Numerical Data** — Çetinkaya-Rundel & Hardin, 2024 · https://openintro-ims.netlify.app/explore-numerical
  Open textbook — modern, classroom-tested treatment of numerical summaries; good primary teaching reference for §1.
  Used for: §1
- **Kurtosis as Peakedness, 1905–2014. R.I.P.** — Westfall, P., 2014 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4321753/
  Peer-reviewed paper — authoritative correction of the "peakedness" myth; use when teaching what kurtosis actually measures.
  Used for: §1
- **Measures of Location** — NIST, 2012 · https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda351.htm
  Official NIST handbook — concise reference definitions for mean, median, trimmed mean; trustworthy but dry.
  Used for: §1
- **Measures of Scale** — NIST, 2012 · https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/eda/section3/eda356.htm
  Official NIST handbook — companion to location; solid for variance, SD, and robust scale measures.
  Used for: §1
- **Principles of Data Science — Measures of Center** — OpenStax, 2023 · https://openstax.org/books/principles-data-science/pages/3-1-measures-of-center
  Open textbook — accessible intro to measures of center; fine for student-level review.
  Used for: §1
- **Skewness — Definition, Examples, Formula** — Scribbr, 2023 · https://www.scribbr.com/statistics/skewness/
  SEO-oriented study-help site — thin secondary definition of skewness; weak for anything beyond a quick formula reminder; prefer OpenIntro/NIST.
  Used for: §1
- **6.041 Probabilistic Systems Analysis, Lecture 1: Probability Models and Axioms** — MIT OpenCourseWare, 2010 · https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-041-probabilistic-systems-analysis-and-applied-probability-fall-2010/fee036e2e13527166fb7f8bcd0647844_MIT6_041F10_L01.pdf
  University lecture notes (MIT OCW) — axioms and probability models from a classic course; good formal grounding.
  Used for: §2
- **Conditional Probability, Explained Visually** — Powell, V., 2015 · https://setosa.io/ev/conditional-probability/
  Interactive visual explainer — excellent intuition for conditioning; not a substitute for a textbook on the algebra.
  Used for: §2
- **Independence and Mutual Exclusivity as Distinct Tools** — Rossman, A., 2008 · https://doi.org/10.1080/10691898.2008.11889578
  Peer-reviewed education paper — clarifies a common student confusion; worth the cite when teaching independence vs disjointness.
  Used for: §2
- **Introduction to Probability, 2nd ed. (Harvard Stat 110)** — Blitzstein, J. & Hwang, J., 2019 · https://probabilitybook.net/
  Textbook — primary reference for the pebble/sample-space framing used in the lesson; strong overall foundation.
  Used for: §2
- **Introduction to Probability, Chapter 1** — Bertsekas, D. & Tsitsiklis, J., 2008 · http://athenasc.com/Prob-2nd-Ch1.pdf
  Textbook chapter (PDF) — clean axiomatic treatment; good formal companion to Blitzstein.
  Used for: §2
- **OpenIntro Statistics — Conditional Probability** — Diez, D., Çetinkaya-Rundel, M. & Barr, C., 2019 · https://stats.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Introductory_Statistics/OpenIntro_Statistics_(Diez_et_al)./02%3A_Probability/2.02%3A_Conditional_Probability_I
  Open textbook (LibreTexts) — solid intro treatment of conditional probability; aligns with the OpenIntro IMS style from §1.
  Used for: §2
- **Seeing Theory — Compound Probability** — Kunin, D. et al., 2018 · https://seeing-theory.brown.edu/compound-probability/index.html
  Interactive educational visualization (Brown) — good for compound-event intuition; light on formalism.
  Used for: §2
- **Using Contingency Tables to Calculate Probabilities** — Frost, J., 2023 · https://statisticsbyjim.com/probability/contingency-tables-probabilities/
  Practitioner stats blog — clear worked examples of joint/marginal/conditional from two-way tables; appropriate for the table walkthrough.
  Used for: §2
